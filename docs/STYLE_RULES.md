# Style rules for DramaBox-style vertical short drama

Craft rules distilled from watching commercially released vertical short-drama
reference (DramaBox-style serialized micro-drama, 9:16, ~90 s episodes). These
are production rules, not a copy of any one show's plot or dialogue.

Apply to every episode plan:

1. **Pacing.** Shots 1.5–2.5 s, about 30–40 cuts/min. Never hold a line more than 3 s on one face; cut to a reaction mid-sentence. Short clips are also easier for Wan to keep clean.
2. **Hook.** First 3 s = pure visual spectacle (a charged moment from later in the episode or series; flash-forward is OK). By 10 s a taboo or stakes line. By 30 s a life-or-death or betrayal stake.
3. **Shot mix.** About 75% CU / ECU (eyes, lips; hands only as approved inserts), about 20% tight two-shots or mediums, under 5% wides and only as 1–2 s establishing inserts.
4. **Camera never locked off.** Slow handheld drift or slow push-in on every shot; push-in on reveals.
5. **Dialogue.** Tight 3/4 or profile shot/reverse, a blurry foreground shoulder as the frame edge. Generate a dedicated ~1 s reaction (gulp, glare, eyes widen) for every threat or flirt line. Put spoken lines on the listener, an over-the-shoulder shot with the speaker's mouth hidden, or a faceless insert — not on an unproven lip-sync close-up.
6. **Romance tension (no face merge).** Faces may be inches apart but must not overlap (keep a measurable gap so two-shots do not merge). ECU inserts of a collar, a shirt being gripped, parted lips, locked eyes, minimal blinking. Hands in inserts stay off faces.
7. **Reveals via prop inserts** (phone glow, a photo, a ring, a newspaper) plus a sound sting, not exposition. Do not ask the video model to render readable text; burn captions and phone UI in assembly.
8. **Violence or shock.** A 0.5 s flash, then an instant reaction cut.
9. **Episode endings (~90 s).** End on an uncompleted action, a shocking demand or ultimatum, or a new character entering frame.
10. **Look.** Glossy commercial grade, strong rim light separating characters from dark backgrounds, catchlights in the eyes. Prompt add: "cinematic rim lighting, glossy, sharp focus, 85mm lens".
11. **Text.** Burned-in centered subtitles, bright yellow with a black outline. Serif name cards fading in briefly when a main character first appears.
12. **Movement.** Z-axis (step toward or away from the lens, lean in or back), not lateral walks across frame. This fits the motion-first rule and avoids walking-hands problems.

## Romance / kiss staging

Kisses, if the series allows them:

13. Profile or OTS angle, CU / ECU, lips centered (crop the forehead). One face may be turned 3/4 away. Hands go on the neck, waist or shoulders and stay off faces. Kiss shots are 3–7 s; seductive beats linger longer (~4.5 s average) than tension cuts (2.5 s).
14. Build and payoff: a threat or approach shot, then the kiss, then a reaction (an onlooker, or one partner breaking away with a line).
15. Near-kiss tension: noses almost brushing (not overlapping), a pull-in by a prop (a bandage end, a tie, a collar), a neck kiss with the head tilted back.
16. Sexy looks: the dominant character holds eye contact looking slightly down, while the other averts, flutters and looks up. Glossy lips catch the light. Approaches run 10–15% slow motion. Breathing sits above the music; a heartbeat SFX can play under the tension.
17. Color code: warm amber, red or candlelight ONLY for desire scenes; cool blue-grey for danger and grief. Shallow depth of field with bokeh.
18. Open in media res with the episode's hottest moment, then hard-cut to "earlier". End on an interruption of a big moment at the highest stakes.
    Non-explicit: no nudity and no sex. Skin and lingerie are OK.

## Story-engine rules (multi-episode)

19. Narrative loop: open on a hook, show a styled time card ("Three Days Ago", red cursive), then build until the timeline catches up to the hook and re-contextualizes it. Use this loop across a multi-episode arc too.
20. Rules made to be broken: a lead sets explicit terms, and the audience waits for each to break. Plant one rule early in each arc.
21. Power must swing every 1–2 episodes: leverage (money, a secret), a counter-demand, a public defense by the love interest.
22. A rival or ex can trigger jealousy. Prefer a phone-screen insert over a full flashback — cheaper and stronger when the model cannot be trusted with readable text (burn the UI in assembly).
23. Catharsis beats: the love interest intercepts an attack and publicly chooses the lead.
24. Rescue at the worst moment, a bold entrance, a carry out.
25. Use location aerials (1–2 s) as transitions between settings.
26. End on a verbal surrender or confession line, cut on the word.

Top addictive patterns: rescue entrance, ruthless protect act, contract/rules negotiation, phone-video jealousy, defending the lead against an ex.
